For a citizen dividend.

A citizen dividend would distribute, for example, at least 1% of total yearly tax collection to all citizens.

Citizen dividends would make democracy stronger, due to more political power resulting from more cash. They could increase wealth in the long term: poor people have an easier time making high-ROI investments — for example, someone can buy painkillers and better food, and as a result work longer and more efficiently. Of course, it is possible that this would reduce growth, due to less efficient capital allocation; one can see the writing of the blogger Scott Alexander on this topic.

It enables people to participate in the economic prosperity of society and the nation.

It could also shelter people from unwanted effects of automation (or immigration).

One can also consider a redistributive wealth tax — for example, a 0.1% yearly tax on total wealth, distributed to everyone. That way, wealth concentration would be reduced instead of increased.

Right now people feel economic prosperity only indirectly, and wealth inequality is sharply rising. It is not clear why this should be the case in a democracy. If a nation is rich and prospering, shouldn't people share in it?

Such a dividend could also encourage people to contribute to the prosperity of the nation.

A more controversial take is to sell the country's stocks — financial papers that entitle the holder to some micro-fractions of all tax revenue (or revenue after some costs). Such papers could be sold, issued, and so on. Right now one can only invest in the stock market. There are some reasons why a country does not engage too much in the economy, but a little bit could be possible.

Of course communism is bad, but right now some nations are at the other extreme. Societies pay something like dividends to old pensioners — but why should all other people be left out? It somehow feels that one becomes a true citizen at retirement age...

Also, voting power could be shared more globally. Today, it would be easy to allow citizens more voting power.

On this topic, there is the Trusted Profile signature. This technology allows one to sign some electronic documents and verify them. Using some cryptography, one could create a service that allows for voting. One would use a code and signature to generate a cryptographic hash or signature, then use it to generate a pseudonymous identity (this way, to prevent a national ID number leak). Then a simple website, and one can have nationwide voting tied to identity.

Another option for identity (probably worse) is to have an email bot: an .edu email sent to the bot's email address would give one identity in response.

One can try to create citizen dividends from the bottom, as charity. The previous methods give us identities. So one can set up crypto addresses, then distribute crypto to all of those addresses. So there would be some pool of money, a list of active addresses, and some code that distributes the money. Something like: each month, 5% of the total money goes to all addresses in the list (equal money to each address). An address needs to be reactivated once every two years or so. Then donations to the address would be widely redistributed.
