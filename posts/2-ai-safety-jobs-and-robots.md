I noticed — heh, what I noticed: I don't understand one thing about the exponential growth of robots.

Feeding a human costs around $100 per month, so $1.2k a year. Let's round it up to $2k a year. Let's say a robot costs $50k in total, plus some operating costs — so about 25 years of human food.

So if there were a sharp increase in robots, we should expect economic competition from humans. Wages can fall to the point of being competitive — anywhere close to that $2k a year. It can be similar with office work.

People can of course still push mass production, but economics doesn't seem to be so clear on that.
